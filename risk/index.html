What you are risking

Signing the declaration risks nothing. Committing money is a different decision, and this page is the part nobody enjoys writing.

Don't commit money unless you are prepared for its value to fall, possibly by a lot, with no way to act while it is committed.

That is the honest version. There is no compensation scheme behind this, no protection if it goes wrong, and nobody to complain to.

This is not an investment

You are not buying a share, a token or a right to anything. There is no return and there never will be: no dividend, no profit share, no payout of any kind. All that ever comes back is your own money, unspent.

If any part of you hopes to make money here, do not take part.

Bitcoin falls as well as rises

It has halved before and it will again. If the price drops while your money is locked, you cannot do anything about it until your lock ends.

That is the price of a commitment the blockchain enforces. Falls have lasted two and three years, which is longer than a one-year lock.

So do not lock money you might need, or money you could not bear to watch fall by half. Pick your lock length with that in mind, not to look impressive on a register.

You are responsible for your own money

Nobody holds your coins. That is the strength of this design, and it means nobody can help if you lose access to them.

If you lose the key to your wallet, your bitcoin is gone. There is no password reset, no account recovery and no support line. Not us, not anybody.

If you have never held bitcoin, learn how backups work before you commit anything, and practise with a small amount first. The movement will still be here.

Backing a purchase cannot be taken back

Locking money and backing a purchase are two separate decisions. The second has no undo.

When you sign your locked money into a proposal, you cannot un-sign it. There is no withdrawal, no cooling-off and no cancel. A proposal has up to four weeks to gather and up to two more to go through, so the most you are bound for is six weeks, and usually less, because gathering stops the moment enough money is behind it. But while it is open, you are bound. Do not back something you are not sure about.

There is a tail to this. A commitment stays valid for as long as the coins it points at have not moved, so a dishonest co-signer could complete one from a proposal that already closed unfunded. It could only go to the destination you signed for, which is on a published list, so it would be an early payment to a named recipient, not theft. Your protection is your lock's date: once it passes you can move the coins yourself, and every old commitment over them dies.

So a five-year lock carries a longer tail than a one-year lock. Longer locks count for longer and expose you for longer. That trade is yours to make.

About those figures. The bitcoin fees go to miners and are the same wherever you are. The pounds are a rough guide at recent prices, so convert to your own currency. What an exchange charges differs from country to country, and we cannot tell you what it will be where you are.

None of it comes to us. We take nothing, charge nothing and earn nothing.

Small amounts are poor value. On the equivalent of £100, fees might be eight to thirteen per cent. On £500 they are a couple of per cent. Fewer, larger locks cost far less than many small ones, and one five-year lock costs one fee where five yearly locks cost five.

It may simply fail

Nobody has done this before. Any one of these could stop it, for a while or for good:

We never raise enough to buy anything meaningful.

No owner is ever willing to sell a stake to a body like this.

Football's regulators decide a movement with anonymous members cannot hold a stake in a club.

A solicitor finds a flaw in the legal structure that cannot be worked around.

Interest fades and nobody renews their lock.

In every one of those cases the outcome is the same: locks run to their dates and every supporter's money comes back to them, unspent. There is no pot to lose, no company to go bust and no administrator to appoint, because your money was never anywhere but your own wallet.

You would have tied up some money for a while, got it back, and had nothing to show for it but the attempt. That is the realistic worst case, and it is also the most likely one.

Risks in the software

The code that builds the locks has been written and tested, including on the real Bitcoin network. Tested code is not the same as safe code.

At the time of writing, nobody outside this project has reviewed it. Tools for finding flaws in code, including AI, are improving fast, for attackers as well as reviewers. A mistake in how a lock is built could leave coins stuck: not stolen, but unspendable. That code needs eyes that are not ours before anyone commits a meaningful sum, and anyone thinking of a large amount should wait for that.

Everything is published so that it can be checked rather than believed. That is not the same as it having been checked.

Publishing everything has one cost. Every lock's keys are on the register, so anyone can check the locks without trusting us. Most ordinary bitcoin addresses keep their key hidden until the coins are spent. Ours don't, so they lose a layer of protection that would matter if someone built a computer powerful enough to work out a private key from a public one. Quantum computers may one day do that. Nobody knows when, and Bitcoin will need a safer kind of address before then. Every lock here ends after five years at most, so locks can move to that safer kind when they are renewed. Until then, ours would be exposed sooner than most.

Tax is yours

In many countries, spending bitcoin counts as disposing of it, and that can be taxable. What your country does is yours to find out. The rules differ everywhere and we cannot tell you. If your locked bitcoin goes into a purchase, treat it as something you must account for until you know otherwise.

Getting your money back is different. Taking a lock home moves coins from one address you control to another. You hold the same bitcoin as before: nothing has been sold or spent. Selling it for cash afterwards is your own separate decision.

We do not collect your details, so we cannot and will not report anything for you. Keep your own records.

And the obvious one

Nothing on this site is legal or financial advice, and nobody involved in writing it is qualified to give you any. If a decision here matters to your finances, speak to somebody who is.

If any of this makes you want to walk away, the page has done its job. Signing the declaration still costs nothing and commits you to no payment, ever.
