Commit

When a purchase is proposed, you can commit money you have locked to it. You sign a payment to the seller from your own lock. It can't move unless the proposal reaches its target and the fund's co-signer adds the second signature. If it doesn't, your money stays locked and comes home on its date as usual.

Before you sign

A commitment holds until the proposal closes, and can't be withdrawn. Nothing moves unless the proposal reaches its target and the co-signer completes it. If it doesn't, your money stays locked and comes home on its date. Your Ledger will show the payment to the seller: approve it only if the address matches the one above.

How this works

A proposal names a seller already on the fund's published list of destinations, a price, a target in bitcoin and its dates. Each commitment is a payment from one member's lock to that seller, signed by the member and missing the co-signer's signature, so on its own it is worthless. Commitments are posted to the fund's relays and kept in its repository in the order they arrived. When the target is met, the co-signer completes them in that order, up to the target and no further, and the payments go out a few at a time. If it isn't met, the co-signer signs a public notice saying so, and nothing ever moves.

Everything shown here is checked in your browser: each proposal against its own fingerprint, and each commitment against the lock it spends, before it is counted. The rules are in the documents.
