What is counted, and what is not

Only bitcoin that is locked, confirmed, still in date and above the minimum, and only the members who hold it.

Signing the declaration is a member giving their word, and every signature is published in full below. This page does not count them. A signature costs nothing to make, and the only figures worth putting here are ones nobody can fake.

Why this is here

A lock opens two ways: by its owner alone once its date has passed, or by its owner and a second signer together at any time. That second signer is called the co-signer, and the second way is meant only for a purchase.

Bitcoin cannot enforce "only for a purchase". A co-signer willing to sign anything could let members take counted bitcoin back early, and the total above would mean nothing. What Bitcoin does record, for everyone to see, is which way each lock was opened. So every lock opened early with the co-signer's signature, other than to pay a listed destination, is shown here. Nobody can keep one off this list, the co-signer included.

How to get on it

Sign the declaration with a bitcoin wallet, then send in the two lines it gives you. It costs nothing, moves no money, and takes about five minutes.

Other ways on, and why they matter

There are two other ways on, and they count exactly the same, because a signature cannot be forged and so needs nobody's permission to pass on. You can open a pull request adding your record to registrations.json, or hand your two lines to anybody willing to publish them for you. Both are on the joining page. They are what stops the button being a gate anyone can shut on you.

How records get here, and what stops them being changed

The joining page posts your signed record to public relays, which are open message boards on a network called Nostr. This repository's own job collects new records every two minutes and checks each signature again before writing it in.

Every change to the register is public in the repository's history, and anyone can keep a complete copy. A copy of the register is also timestamped into the Bitcoin blockchain automatically. Those snapshots are in the anchors/ folder, and each proves that the register said what it said by that time.

Be clear about the limit. The register is not impossible to alter: the person who controls the repository could rewrite its history. Between snapshots, "nobody could quietly remove you" rests on somebody having kept a copy. Keep one.

Run your own copy

This page is static. It reads one file and checks it in your browser, and no server decides what you see. So you can run the identical register yourself, and if your total ever disagrees with ours, ours is wrong.

git clone https://github.com/Jimbus9/the-kop-fund
cd the-kop-fund
node tests/publishable.test.mjs   # the pages load only files that are published
python3 -m http.server            # then open localhost:8000/register/

The checking code's own tests are in the method's repository, github.com/Jimbus9/fanfund. The commands are on the documents page.
