What is counted. Only bitcoin that is locked, confirmed, still in date and above the minimum, and only the members who hold it. Signing the declaration is a member giving their word, and every signature is published in full below. This page does not count them, because a signature costs nothing to make, and the only figures worth putting here are ones nobody can fake.

The co-signer's record

Checking…

Read from the blockchain in your browser.

Why this is here. A lock opens two ways: by its owner alone once its date has passed, or by its owner and the co-signer together at any time. The second is meant only for a purchase, but Bitcoin can't enforce that — a co-signer willing to sign anything could let members take counted money back early, and the total above would mean nothing. What Bitcoin does record, for everyone to see, is which way each lock was opened. So every lock opened early with the co-signer's signature, other than to pay a listed destination, is shown here. Nobody can keep one off this list, the co-signer included.

Check a signature yourself

Paste an address and a signature and this page will tell you whether that address really signed the declaration. Useful if you have just signed and want to confirm it before submitting, and useful for checking anybody else's.

How to get on it

Sign the declaration with a bitcoin wallet, then submit the two lines it gives you. It costs nothing, moves no money, and takes about five minutes. The joining page walks through the whole thing, including what to do if you have no wallet yet, and checks your signature in your own browser before anything is sent.

Join the register

There are two other ways in, and they count exactly the same, because a signature cannot be forged and so needs nobody's permission to pass on: open a pull request adding your record to registrations.json, or hand your two lines to anybody willing to publish them for you. Both are listed on the joining page. They matter: they are what stops the button above being a gate anyone can shut on you.

Submissions are pull requests on purpose, but be clear about what that does and does not give you. Every change is timestamped in public, and anyone can keep a complete copy of the register, so an alteration is detectable by anybody holding one. It is not append-only: git history can be rewritten, and the person who controls the repository can do it. Until each day's register is anchored to the blockchain — which is planned and not yet built — "nobody could quietly remove you" rests on somebody having kept a copy, not on proof. Keep one.

Run your own copy

This page is static. It reads one file and checks it in your browser — there is no server deciding what you see. So you can run the identical register yourself, and if your total ever disagrees with ours, ours is wrong.

git clone https://github.com/Jimbus9/the-kop-fund
cd the-kop-fund
node method/js/verify.test.mjs     # check the verifier against another library
node method/js/crowdlock.test.mjs  # check the Crowdlock files, needs no installs
python3 -m http.server      # then open localhost:8000/register/
