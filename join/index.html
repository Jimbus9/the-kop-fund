You are signing the Supporters' Declaration — read it first if you have not. In short: supporters buying a stake in Liverpool Football Club, with nobody ever holding anybody else's money, and nothing ever paid out to any member.

What is about to happen

Five steps, and the whole thing takes about five minutes. Nothing leaves your device until the last one.

  1. Get a bitcoin wallet — skip this if you already have one.
  2. Copy one line of text from this page.
  3. Sign that line in your wallet. This is a built-in feature called "sign message". It is not a payment.
  4. Paste the result back here. Your own browser checks it and tells you straight away whether it worked.
  5. Press the button to go on the register.

If you get stuck at any step, nothing is lost and nothing has been sent. Close the page and come back.

1 Get a wallet, if you have not got one

A bitcoin wallet is an app that holds your keys. You need one you control yourself — an account at an exchange will not work, because the exchange holds the keys rather than you, and most will not sign a message at all.

You do not need to put any money in it. A brand new, empty wallet signs just as well as one holding a fortune.

On a computer: Sparrow

Free, open source, and the one these instructions know best. Download it from sparrowwallet.com. When it asks, choose a Native Segwit wallet — that gives you an address starting bc1q, which is what the register accepts.

On a phone: BlueWallet

From your app store. Make a new wallet and it will be the right type by default.

If you may put money behind this: a hardware wallet

A small dedicated device — a Ledger or a Trezor, usually somewhere around £50 to £80 or the local equivalent — that keeps the key off your computer entirely. It is the right place for a key that will one day own locked money, and it signs this statement just as well as anything else.

You do not need one to join, and buying one is not a condition of anything. But it is worth knowing now rather than later, for the reason below.

Worth ten seconds of thought before you pick, because this choice is stickier than it looks. The key you sign with is the key that would own your locks if you ever commit money. So use a wallet you mean to keep and have written the recovery words down for — not one you will wipe next month, and not a throwaway you would later want to move off. You can always register a second address later, but you would be a second member rather than the one you started as. Nobody loses anything by that; it is just tidier to start where you intend to stay.

3 Sign it in your wallet

Signing a message is not a transaction. It never touches the blockchain, costs no fee, and works on a wallet that has never held a penny. All it proves is that you hold the key.

Before you put money behind it

    
Signing is free and commits you to nothing. Committing money is the decision with consequences, and the risks page is short, specific and not reassuring. Read it before, not after.

    
You will need bitcoin you already hold, or time to get some. Locking means sending bitcoin out of your own wallet, so it has to be in there first. Buying it for the first time is not instant anywhere: expect to prove who you are, wait on a bank transfer, and in some countries wait again before anything can be moved out of the account. How long depends entirely on where you live and who you buy from — it can be an afternoon, it can be a week. That is their process, not ours, and nothing on this page can shorten it.

    
So if you are starting from nothing: sign now, sort the money out in your own time. The two steps are deliberately separate and there is no deadline on the second. Nobody is counted as having contributed until their money is locked, and nobody is chased.
