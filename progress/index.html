How much is locked, and by how many, is not written here on purpose. Those numbers are on the register, where they are counted from the blockchain in your own browser. A figure typed onto this page would be a claim. A figure counted there is a fact. Expect the real one to be small.

None of the open items affects the safety of bitcoin that has been locked. All of them affect whether this ever achieves anything. This is a very early thing: some documents, some working code that nobody outside the project has reviewed, and a handful of people at most.

Things we got wrong and changed

Published because a design that has never been corrected has usually never been checked.

We were going to pool the money

The first version had one shared treasury, with elaborate rules to stop anyone draining it. Testing found seven ways to break it, and two of them lost everything. The rules were not the problem. Pooling was. Removing it removed every one of those attacks at once.

We then removed locks entirely

For a while the plan was to count supporters' live balances, because a lock seemed like needless friction. That was wrong: a balance proves somebody owns bitcoin, not that they have committed any. Locks came back as the thing that counts, not as a barrier to joining.

We nearly promised never to change the declaration

Dropped, because the first solicitor to read it may say something must be worded differently, and a document that calls itself permanent and then changes looks worse than one that never claimed it. Each version is dated and fingerprinted, and a signature belongs to the version it was made over.

Every change is in the public record of changes, and the specifications are published in full. The documents are here.

This is not an investment. There is no return, no dividend and no payout, now or ever. Bitcoin rises and falls, and bitcoin you lock can fall in value while it is locked. Nothing here is legal or financial advice.

An independent initiative by supporters of Liverpool Football Club. Not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to Liverpool Football Club, its owners, or any official supporters' organisation.

The Kop Fund is a Crowdlock: a published set of rules for a crowd locking bitcoin towards buying something, with nobody holding anybody else's money. Source and documents on GitHub. Questions, problems or ideas: email hello@thekopfund.org or open an issue.
